Dae-Hwan Kim, M.S.

I am a researcher at the Korea Institute of Science and Technology (KIST). My previous work focused on cancer cachexia, drug-induced remodeling of the muscle niche, and the mechanisms of muscle atrophy.

I am currently interested in muscle stem cell aging, stem cell–niche interactions, and how exercise shapes muscle regeneration.

By integrating epigenomics, transcriptomics, and proteomics, I aim to understand how diverse cell populations interact within skeletal muscle and contribute to healthy aging. Ultimately, I hope to translate these insights into strategies that promote healthy aging.

Two preludes

One research direction, built from two beginnings

The story is not a clean switch from one field to another. It is a widening path: physiological stress first, molecular mechanism next, and muscle stem cell aging ahead.

Prelude I

Kyung Hee University: learning what the body does under stress

In the Environmental Exercise Physiology Lab, I trained through Physical Education, Sports Medicine, and Exercise Physiology. The work centered on human-subject physiology, including ischemic preconditioning, ambulatory blood pressure, breathing resistance during exercise, cutaneous thermal sensitivity, and cold adaptation.

This period produced six journal articles, including first-author and co-first-author work. It taught participant management, experimental control, statistical analysis, project coordination, and manuscript writing.

The question that stayed with me: measuring what happens is not enough if I cannot explain why it happens.

Prelude II

Korea University and KIST: learning mechanism through muscle wasting biology

At KU-KIST and KIST, I moved into biology through cancer cachexia, a disease context where skeletal muscle undergoes severe molecular and functional remodeling. My manuscript-stage work integrates public skeletal muscle bulk RNA-seq, single-nucleus RNA-seq, LC-MS/MS proteomics, and in vivo validation.

The project required building analysis capacity instead of stopping at outsourced data. I worked with Linux, R, Python, kallisto, DESeq2, Seurat, Harmony, secretome prediction, FragPipe, DIA-NN, proDA, and statistical modeling, while also gaining hands-on mouse and cell-based experimental experience.

The shift sharpened my goal: use data to find biological meaning, then test that meaning in real biological systems.

Next chapter

Muscle stem cell aging: finding meaning in aging datasets

I am building toward research on how aging and exercise reshape muscle stem cell quiescence, activation, DNA damage responses, niche signaling, and regenerative capacity.

The aim is to move beyond extracting gene lists. I want to use multi-omics and bioinformatics to identify mechanistic questions that deserve wet-lab validation.

Future research focus

Muscle stem cell aging as the next biological question

I want to investigate how aging and exercise reshape the muscle stem cell niche, quiescence, activation, DNA damage responses, and regenerative capacity.

The goal is to use complex aging datasets as a starting point for biological meaning: not just to extract signals, but to decide which mechanisms deserve experimental validation.
