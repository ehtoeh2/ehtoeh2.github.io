History

Two starting points, one research journey

My research journey has been shaped by two distinct beginnings. It began in exercise physiology, reached a turning point in biology, and has since continued along a new path toward understanding muscle aging.

  1. 01

    Prelude I

    Kyung Hee University

    Learning How the Body Responds to Stress

    At the Environmental Exercise Physiology Lab, I built a foundation in physical education, sports medicine, and exercise physiology. My research focused on human physiology, spanning ischemic preconditioning, ambulatory blood pressure, breathing resistance during exercise, cutaneous thermal sensitivity, and cold adaptation.

    During this period, I published six journal articles, including first-author and co-first-author papers. These experiences allowed me to develop skills across the full research process—from participant management and experimental design to statistical analysis, project coordination, and scientific writing.

    Yet through this work, a deeper scientific curiosity emerged. I wanted to move beyond measuring physiological responses and understand, at the molecular level, why those changes occur.

  2. 02

    Prelude II

    Korea University and KIST

    Beyond Physiology, Into the Molecular World

    My work at Korea University and KIST marked my entry into cellular and molecular biology, with a primary focus on muscle atrophy.

    By combining multi-omics analyses with experimental validation, I identified candidate biomarkers associated with muscle wasting and investigated the molecular mechanisms through which drugs reshape skeletal muscle and its surrounding microenvironment.

    I also contributed to the development of 3D muscle-on-chip systems and various organoid models, gaining a broader understanding of how cells organize into tissues and interact with their local environment.

    Working across computational and experimental research taught me that skeletal muscle remodeling emerges from interactions among diverse cell populations and the surrounding niche.

  3. 03

    Next Chapter

    Muscle Stem Cell Aging

    Finding Biological Meaning in an Age of Data Abundance

    This perspective has drawn me toward muscle stem cells, their interactions with the surrounding niche, and how aging and exercise reshape their function and regenerative capacity.

    As multi-omics datasets continue to expand and AI-assisted analytical tools rapidly evolve, the challenge is no longer simply to generate more data. It is to distinguish signals with genuine biological meaning from the overwhelming volume of information.

    My research vision is centered on integrating multi-omics analysis, data-driven hypothesis generation, and experimental validation. By translating patterns in complex datasets into testable biological insights, I ultimately aim to develop strategies that promote healthy aging and extend healthspan.
